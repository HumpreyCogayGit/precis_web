Seven things to fix,
and the flow they add up to.

The site already scrapes eleven good sources and renders them safely. What it does not yet do is tell you which four of today's forty-two items matter. Everything below is grounded in the current App.jsx, lib/articles.js and the public_articles view.

01

The excerpt is not a summary

The view takes left(body_text, 360) || '…'. So every card shows the article's first 360 characters — which is usually the headline repeated, then a boilerplate intro, cut mid-word. Three cards in the current grid open with the same words as their own titles.

Fix

Summarise once, at scrape time, into a summary column: one sentence, 25 words, stating what happened. This is the single highest-leverage change on the list — every other improvement below is arranging summaries on a page.

02

Every item weighs the same

Featured, Editors Picks and Browse All are three sections of near-identical cards: image, 24px condensed headline, 120-word body. A reader scanning for signal gets forty-two equal claims on their attention and no path through them.

Fix

Three tiers, sharply different: one lead with an image, five numbered briefs with a summary line, then a dense headline-only list. Sixty seconds gets you the lead plus the five.

03

"Editors Picks" is slice(1, 5)

Both that heading and the "Popular on Precis" kicker sit above items two through five by publish date. There are no editors and no popularity data. A reader who notices stops trusting the rest of the labels.

Fix

Either earn the label — rank by how many sources covered the story, which you can compute from the data you already hold — or call the section what it is: "Also today". Ranking by coverage also answers "what's trending" honestly.

04

Two filter systems for one job

Source and Topic appear as selects in the hero and again as chip strips 900px further down, wired to the same state. The chip strip needs its own horizontal scrollbar. Choosing a source in one place silently changes the other, and the result appears off-screen.

Fix

One sticky bar under the masthead. Eleven sources and two topics fit on a line at this width — no scroller. The active filter shows in the masthead itself, and the list re-renders in place.

05

Scraper telemetry is on the page

Each card carries "Captured Sep 1, 2026, 12:09 PM" alongside the publish date. fetched_at is your pipeline's business, not the reader's, and showing both dates makes neither legible. Nothing on the page says what day this edition is.

Fix

A dated masthead — "Tuesday, September 1" — with one honest freshness line under it. Items show relative publish time. Keep fetched_at for sorting; stop printing it.

06

The same story lands as separate cards

There is no clustering, so a launch covered by OpenAI's blog, NVIDIA and Perplexity is three cards a reader has to reconcile. open_ai and open_ai_releases also both render as "OpenAI", which reads as a duplicate in the source list.

Fix

Group by story and show "3 sources" on the row; the cluster page holds the per-source takes in a timeline. Merge the two OpenAI keys behind one display source with a channel label.

07

Nothing brings anyone back tomorrow

No search, no saving, no read state, no email, no permalink for a story. visibleCount resets to twelve on every filter change, so browsing deep and then switching topic loses your place. On a phone the three-column grid stacks into forty-two long cards.

Fix

Search, save-for-later and a morning email, in that order of effort. The archive of dated editions is free once the edition exists — yesterday's brief is a permanent, linkable page.
The flow

Six screens

Headlines and excerpts are real items from your current feed. The one-line summaries are written from those excerpts to show the shape — in production they come from the summarise step in fix 01. Images are placeholders.
